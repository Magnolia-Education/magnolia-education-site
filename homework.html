Homework and Sprints

What it is, why we built it, and how to get the most from it

When your tutor gives you homework, it now shows at the top of your Magnolia portal Home. You accept it, which signs it with your initials, and your tutor checks it at the start of your next lesson. If a task keeps slipping, you can split it into sprints: short, planned blocks of focus across the days before that lesson.

1.Why we built it

Tutors often give extra work in a lesson: a set of practice questions, a video to watch, a study guide to make. That work is not the school's homework. It fills the gaps the class won't, like a review of fractions before the next unit. And until now, it was easy for everyone to lose track of it. The student forgot, the tutor forgot exactly what was said, and the parent never knew it existed.

So every task is now written down in one place, and the student agrees to it. Signing for a task is a small act, but it changes who the plan belongs to. People are more likely to follow through on a commitment they made themselves, especially one with a firm deadline they chose.1

Sprints are for the other half of the problem: getting started. Putting a task off is rarely about laziness. It is usually about the task feeling large, vague or unpleasant.2 Deciding in advance when you will work on it, and keeping each block short, makes starting much easier.3

2.How homework works

  1. Your tutor assigns it, usually during the lesson: what to do, and any links you need. It is due at your next lesson in that subject, or a week later if no lesson is booked yet.
  2. You accept it. It appears at the top of your Home as new homework. Tap Accept and your initials are stamped on it, like signing your name. Until you do, your tutor and your parent see “Not accepted yet”.
  3. You do it before the lesson. Finished sooner than planned? Tap Finished early. Your tutor still checks it.
  4. Your tutor checks it at the start of the lesson. They mark it completed, partially completed, incomplete, or no longer relevant. Unfinished work can carry on to the next lesson, still signed with your initials.
  5. You can add your own. Want to hold yourself to something? Choose Add your own homework on your lesson card. It is signed straight away and checked like any other.

3.Sprints

One sprint is 25 minutes of focus, then a 5 minute break. Make the break an active one: stretch, take the stairs, get a glass of water. Not your phone. The idea comes from the Pomodoro Technique, a method of working in short timed blocks.4

  1. Plan them with your tutor. When your tutor assigns homework, you can choose the days before your lesson to do it on, and how many sprints each day. It works best when you pick the days yourself, so it is your plan.
  2. See your week at a glance. Your sprints sit in a strip of days above your homework, one colour per subject, with your lesson days marked.
  3. Tap a sprint when you finish it. It fills in with a tick. Tapped the wrong one? Tap Undo, or tap it again.
  4. Plans change. Drag a sprint to another day to move it. Did more than you planned? Tap + on a day to add a sprint, then tick it off. You can add sprints to any homework, even one that started without them.
  5. Finish them all. When every sprint for a homework is done, they bloom into magnolias.

Sprints are optional. Your tutor will suggest them when they would help: when homework hasn't been getting done, or when you'd like more structure. Plenty of homework needs nothing more than an Accept.

4.For parents

You see the same homework in your own portal, on your child's Home: each task, whether it has been accepted and with whose initials, and which sprints are done. Only your child can accept homework or tick off a sprint, so the initials always mean them. If a task still says “Not accepted yet” the day after it was set, that is worth a gentle question.

5.Common questions

Can I decline homework?
No. There is only Accept. If something about a task doesn't work for you, talk to your tutor; they can change it at any time.
When is it due?
At your next lesson in that subject, as shown on the homework. With no lesson booked, it is due a week after it was set.
How many sprints can I plan?
Up to 6 a day, on any day from today up to your lesson, across at most a week.
What if I miss a sprint?
Nothing happens on its own. Move it to another day, or just do it late and tick it off. Your tutor sees the plan and the ticks, and you can talk it through at the lesson.
What if I finish early?
Tap Finished early on the homework. The sprints you hadn't done yet are cleared, and your tutor checks the work at your lesson.
Who can see my homework?
You, your parent, your tutor for that subject and the Magnolia team.
